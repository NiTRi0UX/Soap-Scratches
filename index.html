Logic and Trains Concepts

An expansion Concept for the Orbitech Mod. Integrating Node-based Logic and specialized Railway Automation.

Vision Logic Trains Kinetics Experience

1. Project Overview

Logic and Trains Concepts is a sophisticated expansion Concept for the Orbitech mod. It introduces a node-based logic and specialized train automation system to the existing Orbitech kinetic framework.

The goal is to provide players with a "Satisfactory-style" logic infrastructure that moves away from clunky, block-by-block wiring toward a clean, visually intuitive network.

By merging your concepts with Orbitech, players can use the Orbitech base for kinetic power (Handcranks, Waterwheels) while utilizing the Concept for the "nervous system"—wireless communication, logic gates, and rail controls.
▼ The Glimmerchord System

This is a logic system attempting to bring something new to block survival, as well as representing unique concepts allowing it to stand apart from common logic systems in block survival games or mods.

Common Pitfalls of Existing Logic Systems

  • Archaic/Dated interactions - Something that is consistent through block survival games and mods that include logic hinge on the base mechanics of block building games. In most cases that works flawlessly, but in the sense of simple or advanced logic requiring some form of routing signals, this starts to feel clunky and crammed. Placing a wire/dust one at a time leaves much to desire for both adept and beginning players, take for example Minecraft. As a beginner you will have to struggle with the intrusive connections between nearby wires/dust, requiring you to expand logic to absurd sizes to avoid shorts; or inconsistent vertical planning, making it difficult to visibly understand the continuity of the logic. On the other hand an adept player will aspire to build dense logic, computers, cable cars, or music boxes. In the same vein, the player will struggle with the intrusive connections, making big logic builds become larger than multiple chunks; or the vertical planning leaving the player to re-discover visibly confusing logic or consider technical debt.
  • Deep Knowledge Requirements - It becomes apparent very quickly in slightly more complicated logic systems that deep game knowledge is required to have reproducible experiences for building said systems. A good example of this is the processing time of Redstone in Minecraft. Due to its limitation, lack of control, and non existent channels, the player is left with hard computational or timing problems to have logic produce the desired effect on time, or even at all. This leaves beginning players lost, requiring deep diving into server tick time/pules, piston blocking states, quasi-connectivity, instant signal transmission, transparent diodes, and target redirection; If you haven't heard of any of those before, than welcome to the problem... This barrier separates interest into obscurity, those who have the drive will lose it when they have to break the fourth wall to solve their problem.
  • Lack of Creativity - Obviously there are concepts I and many will be providing that lack a fully creative attempt, where there are opportunities to take creative liberties, there is the same requirement to align a mechanism or item to be inline with the public zeitgeist. It inspires many to take an approach that is unique: think Witch Hat Atelier, the magic mechanic itself is rather unique and in some cases hard to approach if you don't have the base understanding of the mechanics it's built on. Glyphs, Drawing, and Magical Circles are basis that we understand. but Witch Hat Atelier takes it a step further, using the basis to build up to a programming like syntax magical system, allowing a staggering amount of variables to modulate the well understood system. This is the realm we need to fit in, allowing the logic to be understandable and approachable by new players, but innovative and unique to expand the adept player.

Intricacies of the System

  • Channels and Inheritance - One plan to address the interactive capabilities and deep knowledge requirements of logic systems is to expand the isolation of logic networks, allowing channels can compact simple and complex logic, as well as reduce the mental tax to plan logic systems to scale. Included in this is an inheritance like translation system, channels will not interfere with each-other, but can be converted easily by altering the color of the Glimmerchord, allowing trees of triggers or sub-logic. There will also be a channel that interacts with all channels, allowing a ring-0 like override to any logic system planned, allowing native hierarchy and control scalability.
  • Node Connections (Point to Point) - To modernize the planning and building of logic systems, I've taken inspiration from smash-hit automation games like Satisfactory and Factorio. These games leave behind the aging block by block wiring system seen in most block survival games and mods. Easily this solves the scalability and cross-talk issues seen in block by block logic systems by forgoing the "physical" placement of wires/dust, allowing a visual clarity in dense logic systems, giving players the ability to debug and plan to scale easily.
  • Wireless Granularity - Remote control of logic is an eventual requirement of simple and complex systems for many reasons, but most automation projects implement this poorly introducing conflicts in control. My approach is to separate remote control of logic into two halves akin to what is seen in real-life. In reality we do not share the control of technology with technologies control, you may control a TV by using a remote, but the TV controls the contents destination using the internet. In the same way I want to separate player remote control from machine remote control, allowing the deepest form of sub-logic governing while removing remote mishaps during operation. This makes a true separation between functionality, and accessibility allowing beginning players to see the barrier to climb for advancement, and a clear path of capabilities for adept players to use the correct tools/items, not apply band aid or roundabout solutions.
  • Computational Control - With the Wireless capabilities mentioned, I want to address an ambitious mechanism planned in these concepts. Like previously mentioned, there is a separation with how we control technology and how technology controls itself, but at the core of this, there is human intention and design. There is programming and micro controllers that dictate how technology should control itself. That is what I want to introduce to the player, a sub-system for simple programming of player remote control, in a way allowing you to program the logic to communicate and function with itself. This will also remove the requirement of deep game knowledge, planning perfect remote timing becomes trivial and will no longer require understanding of server tick time/pulses and advanced exceptions.
  • Sensors & Creative Liberties - There are a few concepts that lack unique creativity, an example would be the Logic Gates (Clusters), obscuring the basic approach to logic gates would create a high barrier of entry, especially for those already trained and experienced on the base concepts. But there are a couple examples that break from this, the Sensors (Sensy-Tenna, Stunted-Tenna, Track-Chime, Chime-Responder) are a unique attempt at redesigning pressure plates and observers. The mechanism of sensing is so common that altering of the concept will not lead to huge confusion, at the same time one has to be sure to relate the functionality to existing logic so players can derive its use. But overall it will separate us from the many iterations of automation in the future, giving granularity and customization when needed to apply sensory stimuli.
  • Holes with Shia LaBeouf - The basis of connecting to logic gates, machines, and various other logic accepting blocks will be complete customization. These receivers of logic will have "Pin Holes", these holes will take any type of pin, any type of antenna, or "others" that may bring custom functionality. Already this opens the door to allowing any logic enabled device to have full compatibility with any of our pin ideas. For example the antennas can provide wireless logic control, or a sensor antenna can be placed to bring item/entity sensing directly to the machine. With all these aspects it even gives modders an avenue to bring interesting capabilities to every part of our logic system.

Restrictions of the System

  • Build & Transmission Length - You will have two ways to link nodes, pins and poles. Pins will allow close connections, somewhere around 12-18 blocks, used primarily to connect logic or machines. For further distances you will have to use a pole, giving you anywhere between 30-50 blocks of length, one will use this to stretch logic signals far distances. that may seem overpowered due to build length being the only barrier, but that is where I'm introducing transmission length. The base transmission will be around 12-18 blocks, allowing logic systems to be wired with no transmission issues. Though, if you want to stretch the signal distances you will need to "Boost" the signal along its way using a "Crystal Intensifier." These boosters will require kinetic speed to "Boost" the signal farther. This isn't just a protection to neuter the transmission, but allows advanced logic systems honoring the real physical parallel. In real circuitry we don't turn a signal off and on to trigger other events, we "Pull" the voltage and look for the drop or rise as the trigger. Mojang understood this and based their logic system on this allowing in-depth logic, like literal computers to be built using Redstone.
  • Remote Insanity - There is a forked approach to the restrictions of wireless control, player remote control will have a distance fall off as the protection for overuse forcing it to primarily take the role of nearby player interaction. Where the machine control will lean on the channel capacity as the protection, This means remote machine control has to be carefully planned to not overwrite existing controls, a common problem we deal with in wireless transmission. Both of these decisions are to force the player to rely on the systems as they are designed, plenty of room for remote machine control of what's important, but not enough to run all gates and logic on wireless. In the same, player remote control will be convenient and capable enough to be the preferred for remote player control, but with enough exposure will lead to the advancement into logic programming.
  • Duplicity vs Obscurity - Through a handful of these concepts, like Logic Gates (Clusters), you will see some redundant items, like XNOR and XOR being modulations of OR, NOR, and NOT. But after deep diving into the use cases of all functions, it gives greater paths of discovery for players to experiment and understand logic concepts using multiple avenues. This also gives superficiality to logic design, removing the requirement of deep game knowledge to get unique logic functions built, instead choosing what fits best in the situation instead of complication trough obscure functionality. This pushed me to redesign antennas as they provided obscure duplicity, same functionality of gates without a real-world parallel or basic understandings, leading the player to confusion by combined functionality. Instead we need duplicity where it makes sense for emergent problem solving but consistent purposeful items and mechanics for learning and experimenting.

In my eyes this system fits itself in the middle of whats familiar and easy to grapple and whats distinct and innovative. This will be approachable by new players who will learn a sprawling in-depth logic system, and constructive for adept player who will adapt to a uniquely capacious logic system.

Here is one of my envisioned scenarios:

A long track comes to a split, to the left ventures out into the badlands. You plan to head deep into zone 2 but your train and stomach are running low on fuel. Upon your arrival to the split you flip a lever on your train allowing the Chime Ear to respond to the Track Chime on the track below. A signal passes up into your Orchestrion, which sounds off in a cacophony of notes. At the split you see an Acoustic Receptor that actives a Track Controller directing you into a nearby station. As you approach, the finishing Orchestrion lets out its final coda activating the fueling procedure. The train halts and a Chute greets your cargo, dumping in the necessary fuel for the journey ahead. A Stunted-Tenna signals the last items arrival, engaging the locomotive to resume its travel. Through logic autonomy you've quickly gathered the resources to survive your travel to zone 2 with little effort, congratulations Orbineer!

2. Core Logic Mechanics

The logic system is built on Point-to-Point Node Connections, designed to solve the "crammed" look of traditional block-based logic.

A. Signal Transmission & Infrastructure

Glimmerchords: Multi-colored wires representing distinct logical channels. Channels allow for parallel logic networks (e.g., a "Red" channel for alarms, "Blue" for lights) without interference.

Nodes (Twine Pines & Poles): Connections are made by clicking on "Pin Holes." This allows for clean, straight-line wiring and 3D routing.

Transmission Limits: Connections have a physical build limit. Long-distance signals require Glimmer Intensifiers, which convert kinetic energy into signal boosting.

Twine Pines: The physical connection point. High customization via pin placement.

Glimmer Mast: Utility poles with built-in pin holes for complex infrastructure.

Glimmer Intensifier: Boosters for signal strength. They convert kinetic speed into transmission boosting.

B. Sensors & Inputs

Sensy (Entity) & Stunted (Item) Tenna: Wireless-capable sensors that detect entities or items passing through their range. They connect to another of the same type up to 5 blocks away.

Cinder & Seeker Tenna: Sending (TX - Red) and receiving (RX - Blue) antennas. They adopt the color channel of the connected glimmerchord and flash when active.

Sensy & Stunted: Detect presence and relay signals wirelessly.

Cinder & Seeker Tenna: Dedicated TX and RX antennas for wireless logic transmission.

Lever & Button: Manual inputs with one pin hole. If no pin/antenna is used, they activate the logic block they are attached to.

C. Logic Gates & Processing

Nexus Clusters (Logic Gates): Logic gates (AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR, XNOR, NOT) with two pin holes for input and output. They can take either a pin or an antenna.

Nexus Clusters: The building blocks of complex, conditional logic. They are designed for modularity and high-density signal processing.

Link Board & Matrix: Multi-pin connectors. The Board has two holes; the Matrix has three. They are used to pair pins with antennas, pair antennas with sensors, or convert channels as repeaters.

D. Player Control & Microcontrollers

Advanced interaction and automation components for the player.

Resonance Horn: The player's remote control. It features four slots for crystals to set channel colors and a radial menu for selection.

Acoustic Receptor: The receiver for the Resonance Horn. It requires a crystal to set the responding channel and has a pin hole for output.

Orchestrion: The "de facto" microcontroller. It takes a "Pull Signal" (up for start, down for stop) and automatically plays a Phonic Score.

Phonic Score: A programming sheet for the Orchestrion. Features a blank inventory to program automatic logic control or music.

3. Train Logic

A dedicated suite of components designed to integrate seamlessly with the Orbitech kinetic network for railway automation.

Rail Router: A train-specific track switcher. It takes a signal over a pin hole and directs a train down a different track (supports 3 states: left, right, middle).

Track Chime & Responder: Devices to pass signals to/from trains traveling above, facilitating remote signaling and rail coordination.

Chute Guard: A logic-controlled gate for chutes. Signal pull-up opens; pull-down closes.

4. Items and Kinetics

Resource processing and the manufacturing pipeline for the Concept.

Manufacturing Pipeline

Silica Powder → Silicon Ingot → Wafer → Etched Wafer → Link Board → Nexus Cluster

Processing Machines & Resources

Geo-Vat: A crystal farm that grows from shards and consumes water.

Kinetic Spinner: Automatically crafts Glimmerchord and other fibers.

Metabolic Agitater: Processes compost into Nitrate, Fertilizer, and Potash.

Heater Core: Automatically bakes Etched Wafers into Link Boards (also cooks food).

Rod-Mil: Automatically turns metal plates into metal tubes, or metal ingots into metal rods.

5. Player Experience

The progression path for players exploring the new logic and train systems.

Beginner Entry Points

  • • Manual Power: Using a Handcrank to power a Kinetic Spinner.
  • • Simple Logic: Using a Lever to turn on a light.
  • • Basic Railway: Using a Track Chime and Chime Ear to send a simple "Pass" signal to a train.

Advanced Play

  • • Wireless Automation: Using Stunted-Tenna sensors to detect full bins and trigger a Packager via a Link Board.
  • • The Orchestrator: Programming an Orchestrion with a "Growth Cycle" score to cycle through machines.
  • • Automated Rail Hubs: Using Rail Routers and Chute Guards to coordinate a fleet of trains.

Build Scenarios

Scenario 1: The Automated Flax Farm

A Waterwheel powers a Kinetic Spinner and a Milling Stone. A Stunted-Tenna detects when the Flax bin is full, signaling a Chute Guard to release the processed Flax.

Scenario 2: The Orchestrated Orchard

A large kinetic network powers various sprinklers and harvesters. The Orchestrion is programmed with a "Growth & Harvest Cycle" score, activating the machines in a specific sequence every 10 minutes.

Scenario 3: The Rail Logistics Hub

A large-scale kinetic network powers the heavy machinery for packing. Rail Routers and Track Chimes coordinate a fleet of trains. Sensors detect cargo types, and Rail Routers direct the trains to the correct "Track."
